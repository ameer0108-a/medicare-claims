Medicare claims warehouse · 2015 to 2022

Where an ACO's Medicare dollars go, and what would actually bring them down

About 8,000 traditional Medicare members, 1.9 million claim lines, and 515,000 pharmacy fills, built into a SQL warehouse in DuckDB. Dollars are Medicare paid amounts. Rates use member months with both Part A and Part B as the denominator.

Synthetic data.  CMS built these files to look like real claims. The people aren't real and some patterns aren't either. The method is the point.

2022 at a glance

Where the money goes

Medical spend per member per month (PMPM) grew 39% from 2015 to 2022. Outpatient dialysis was the fastest growing piece, and members with end-stage renal disease (ESRD) carry a third of all spend.

Who drives it

A small group of members accounts for most of the spend. The question for care management is whether the same people stay expensive next year.

Hospital use

Counted as overnight stays. 73% of inpatient claims in this file start and end the same day and pay under $1,000 at the median, which doesn't match real Medicare, so they stay in spend but not in admission counts.

Medication adherence

Proportion of days covered (PDC) for the three Part D Star adherence measures, using the PQA method. A member counts as adherent at 80% or more. The 60 to 79% band is the outreach list: one or two refills on time gets most of them over the line.

2022 members by PDC band

Statins 81.6% adherent (892 members), RAS antagonists 20.5% (516), diabetes 14.3% (56). Real plans run in the high 80s; the synthetic fill patterns have long gaps.

What would pay off

Two programs priced with low, base, and high assumptions. Pharmacy outreach to the 270 near misses pays back in the base case. Care management for last year's 167 non-ESRD high spenders loses money unless it cuts admissions by 25%, and the best program in Medicare's care coordination trials managed 17%.

About this data

  • Synthetic. CMS's synthetic Medicare files mimic the layout of real research files. Counts and patterns are useful for building and testing logic, not for real conclusions.
  • Medicare Advantage flag. About 60% of fee-for-service claims fall in months where the person also shows a Part C contract, which can't happen in real data. I treated everyone as traditional Medicare. Dropping those months moves 2022 PMPM from $1,665 to $1,692.
  • No ER measure. The outpatient file has no ER revenue codes and the professional file has no ER visit codes or place of service 23.
  • Paid, not allowed. The institutional files don't carry an allowed amount, so every dollar here is what Medicare paid.
  • Checks. 27 reconciliation checks run at the end of every build, from raw row counts to two PDC values worked by hand. All pass. The QA log covers every decision above.
